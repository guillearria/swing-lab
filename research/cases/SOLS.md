## Move
Solstice spent early July giving up roughly a quarter of its value across two enormous-volume
sessions, then ground lower for seven more weeks to a level it had not seen since the spin.
On the last complete bar of this run it gapped up and held, recovering a little over a third of
what the July break took — leaving most of the derating intact.

## Why
The July break was the announcement of a cash-and-stock acquisition of Element Solutions, a
target valued at more than the acquirer itself. The market did what it does to an acquirer
paying up with a levered balance sheet eight months after being spun out: it took the multiple
down. On 2026-08-27 both boards mutually terminated the merger agreement — **no break fee
either way** — citing shareholder feedback, and the Solstice board authorised the company's
first share repurchase programme, funded from cash on hand and operating cash flow. The Q2
beat and raised full-year guidance from late July are untouched by any of this.

So two things changed at once, in opposite directions from the July event: the liability the
market was discounting no longer exists, and a new, mechanical, price-insensitive buyer does.

## The pattern (why this is reusable)
**When a market derates an acquirer for a specific announced deal, that discount is a
LIABILITY with a known price — the announcement-window drop. If the deal dies cleanly, the
liability is extinguished, and the gap between the announcement drop and the termination
recovery is a measurable, falsifiable claim rather than a feeling.** Two conditions make it
readable: (1) the announcement drop must be unambiguously deal-driven — enormous volume,
two sessions, no other news — so the size of the discount is not guesswork; (2) the
termination must be CLEAN — no break fee, no litigation, no financing condition left hanging —
or the discount is being replaced by a different liability instead of removed.

The capital-return announcement that so often accompanies a termination is the confirming
tell, not the thesis: it is management saying the cash is coming back to holders rather than
going into another target.

## What kills it
The market may be discounting the MANAGEMENT, not the deal — a board that attempts a
transformational acquisition within a year of the spin can be re-rated for its
capital-allocation judgement, and that discount survives the deal's death. A recovery that
stalls well short of the pre-announcement price, with no new deal announced, is the tell that
this is what happened.
